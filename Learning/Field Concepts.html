Field Concepts

Surveying, wells and area-table ideas that come up on wellsite plans, in plain words, with how each one shows up in IGI drawings.

Survey & coordinates

Combined Scale Factor (CSF)

The drawing measures on a flat map grid; the plan must report what you'd measure on the actual ground. CSF is the one number that converts between the two.

Why we need it

The Earth is curved but coordinate grids are flat, so a tape distance on site (ground) differs slightly from the distance between coordinates in the drawing (grid). Tiny, but our tables print areas to 0.001 ha, so it changes the numbers.

Three surfaces, two corrections

GroundThe physical Earth, where the crew measures
EllipsoidA smooth mathematical model of the Earth
GridA flat coordinate system (UTM, Alberta 3TM). Our drawings are grid.

Ground × Elevation Factor → ellipsoid, × Grid Scale Factor → grid. CSF rolls both into one multiplier:

CSF = EF × GSF

Typical values: about 0.9995 to 1.0001.

Which way to convert

DirectionFormulaWhen
Ground → gridGrid = Ground × CSFPutting a crew's measurement into the drawing
Grid → groundGround = Grid ÷ CSFReporting from the drawing (what our area tables do)
AreasGround area = Grid area ÷ CSF²Area is length × width, so both get corrected

1,000 m on the ground at CSF 0.999500 is 999.500 m on the grid: 50 cm in one kilometre.

Ground area for the table–
In IGI drawings. CSF is stored in the USERR1 system variable (type USERR1 to see it). igl-get-combined-scale-factor reads it rather than the CSF drawing property. ARTS, AWUW and IAT compute ha = m² ÷ 10,000 ÷ CSF². DAREA doesn't apply CSF, so its areas are grid, not ground.

Checklist

  • Is CSF already applied? Don't apply it twice or miss it.
  • Direction: ÷ for grid → ground. Some workflows store the reciprocal.
  • Areas use CSF², not CSF.
  • CSF is not the plot or viewport scale (1:500, 1:1000).
Practice questions

1. The crew measures 2,000 m. CSF 0.999800. Grid larger or smaller? → Smaller: 1,999.600 m (0.400 m less).

2. A lease is 2.500 ha in the drawing, USERR1 = 0.99950. Table value? → 2.500 ÷ 0.99900 = 2.503 ha (6.18 ac).

3. DAREA says 2.500 ha for the same lease. Why? → It skips CSF², so it reports grid area.

Survey & coordinates

Grid vs ground distance

Grid is what you measure between coordinates in the drawing. Ground is what a tape on site would read. They differ by the CSF.

Ground = Grid ÷ CSF. See Combined Scale Factor.

Survey & coordinates

Elevation Factor (EF)

Higher ground sits farther from the Earth's centre, so the same angle covers a longer distance up there. EF shrinks ground distance down to the ellipsoid.

EF = R ÷ (R + h)

R ≈ 6,371,000 m (Earth's radius); h = ellipsoidal height, not just elevation above sea level. A lease near 800 m gives EF ≈ 0.999874. For all of Alberta, EF is a little under 1.

Part of CSF.

Survey & coordinates

Grid Scale Factor (GSF)

Flattening a curved Earth onto a map always stretches or squashes it, like flattening an orange peel. GSF is how much, at your location.

It's lowest on the projection's central meridian (0.9996 in UTM, 0.9999 in Alberta 3TM) and grows moving east or west, sometimes going above 1 near zone edges.

Part of CSF. See UTM and 3TM.

Wells & naming

Well types: vertical, DD, HZ

The difference is the path the hole takes underground, and it changes the well name.

VerticalDirectional (DD)Horizontal (HZ)
PathStraight downSteered at an angleCurves to about flat (80°+)
Bottom holeUnder the surfaceOffset, often a few hundred mOften 1–3 km away
On the drawingUsually no drill pathShort drill pathLong drill path
NameCVE 100 BERLRW 5-18-59-24CVE 100 DD BERLRW …CVE 100 HZ BERLRW …

An HZ well is technically directionally drilled too, but in names the AER uses DD for angled, not-horizontal wells.

In WNH. One HZ or DD drawing property applies to every line, so a mixed pad gets the wrong type on some lines, and vertical wells with no drill path aren't found. Parked as WNH open question 5.

Wells & naming

Lateral

The flat, horizontal leg of an HZ well that runs through the formation, often 1–3 km long.

See Well types. The drawing is a top-down plan, so it shows how far away the bottom hole is, but not how steep the hole is.

Wells & naming

LSD and legal location

Alberta land is addressed as LSD-Section-Township-Range-Meridian, e.g. 5-18-59-24 W4M.

  • Meridian: W4M = 4th meridian (110° W), W5M = 114° W, W6M = 118° W.
  • Range: counted westward from the meridian, about 6 miles each.
  • Township: counted northward from the US border, 6 miles square, 36 sections.
  • Section: 1 mile square, about 640 acres.
  • LSD (legal subdivision): 1/16 of a section, about 40 acres, numbered 1–16 in a back-and-forth pattern starting at the south-east corner.
In WNH. Bottom-hole LSDs come from the 10-Section Labels plus a 4 × 4 split of each section.

Wells & naming

UWI and 100 / 102 / 103

The Unique Well Identifier. Its leading number tells wells that end in the same LSD apart.

The first well whose bottom hole is in an LSD is 100. The next ones in that same LSD are 102, 103 …, in licence order. Existing wells count first.

Full form: 100/05-18-059-24W4/00.

In WNH. Numbered from all wells, new and existing. A Bottom Hole 102/… label overrides it. The UWI drawing property is ignored because one value can't describe a whole pad. Cenovus always shows 100; Whitecap never does.

Wells & naming

Strike area / field

The AER area the bottom hole falls in. Its name or abbreviation goes in the well name (SMOKY, RESTHA, BERLRW …).

A field order wins over a strike area. One pad can span two areas, so each well is looked up by its own bottom-hole section.

In WNH. Looked up in Well Name Header - Strike Areas.txt, which is built from the AER Order System data. Refresh it monthly.

Area tables

Upland vs wetland

What kind of ground the new lease sits on: dry and firm (upland), or wet most of the year (wetland).

UplandWetland
WhatForest, grassland, farmlandBog, fen, marsh, swamp, shallow open water
Why it mattersNormal constructionAlberta Wetland Policy: may need extra approval, reclamation or compensation
Upland + Wetland = Area Required total

Job 26133: 4.135 + 0.023 = 4.158 ha ✓. All Crown plans need this table, even when wetland is 0.

In IGI drawings. Wetland = the area inside Areas Linework-Wetland. Upland = Area Required total − Wetland. Routine: AWUW.

Area tables

ABMI

The Alberta Biodiversity Monitoring Institute, run with the U of A and U of C. It publishes the province-wide wetland inventory.

That inventory is the source of the wetland linework behind the Upland / Wetland table. Still to confirm: whether every job uses ABMI or sometimes another source.

Incidental activities

Extra, usually temporary, pieces of land needed to build the site, listed separately from the lease itself.

ActivityWhat for
TWSRoom beside the lease for soil piles and equipment
Log deckPiling timber cut during clearing
Borrow pitDigging gravel or fill for the pad
CampsiteA temporary crew camp

Table columns: ID (IA1, IA2 …), activity, latitude, longitude, hectares. A * means it's entirely inside the disposition, so it adds no new land.

In IGI drawings. Workspace* layers. IAL labels each one, and IAT builds the table. Each IA's hectares must match its row in Disposition and Timber Salvage.

Extra room beside the lease, used during construction for soil piles and equipment, then reclaimed.

One kind of incidental activity.

Area tables

Outside / within existing, new / existing cut

Each piece of the new project is split two ways: is the land already granted to someone (existing disposition)? Are the trees already cleared (existing cut)? What's left over is the new part.

Outside Existing = piece − existing disposition inside it
New Cut = piece − existing cut inside it

A piece (component) is one row of Area Required: the site, the access road, a TWS …

Example: 3.000 ha padWithin / ExistingOutside / New
Disposition Areas table1.000 ha overlaps an old lease2.000 ha new land
Timber Salvage table1.200 ha already cleared1.800 ha trees to cut

The two splits are separate questions, so the numbers don't have to match.

In IGI drawings. The existing parts are the shapes on Areas Linework-Existing Disposition and Areas Linework-Existing Cut. ARTS only counts a shape that sits wholly inside the piece. One that crosses the edge is silently ignored, so draw it clipped to the piece.

Area tables

Hectares and acres

1 ha = 10,000 m² = 2.4710538 acres. Our tables show both.

ac = ha × 2.4710538

Rounding: hectares to 0.001, acres to 0.01. Compute from the ground area (after CSF²), not from rounded numbers, or totals can drift by 0.01.

Land & approvals

Disposition

The right to use Crown land that the government grants for the project, such as the wellsite lease (e.g. an MSL) and its access road.

Its pieces are the rows of the Disposition Areas table. Incidental activities are approved with it but listed separately.

Land & approvals

Crown vs freehold land

Crown land is owned by the province; freehold is privately owned.

Crown plans carry all five area tables (Area Required, Disposition, Timber Salvage, Upland/Wetland, Incidental Activities). Freehold plans carry only Area Required.
